# Product

## Platform

One word: web, mobile, desktop, cli.

## Users

One paragraph per kind of user: what they do with it, on which device, how often. Name the people who are *not* users but will see it.

## Product Purpose

What the product is for, and the one job it must do first. Record the date the user confirmed that job.

Success: the single outcome that counts as the product working.

## Positioning

What makes it different, stated as something the product does, not something it says about itself. If the core enforces a rule, state the rule here, so no screen can overstate what is known.

## Operating Context

Where and how it runs: devices, network, what runs in the background, what happens offline, who pays for what, and any monthly cap on external spend.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- What exists today, by screen or by job.
- The language of the interface and of the repo, if they differ.
- Who decides a business rule and who words it (see `CODING_STANDARDS.md`).
- Out of scope, each item with the issue that decided it.
- Undecided, as a list, so that no agent decides it by accident.

## Brand Commitments

Name, voice, design system (link), icon status. A binding decision carries its date and "user-confirmed".

## Evidence on Hand

Real data, real results, real documents the product can point at.

Absences future work must not fill in: no invented testimonials, metrics, prices or users.

## Product Principles

1. Three to five numbered principles, each one sentence an agent can check a change against.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The commitments and the open gaps, with the date of the last audit.
